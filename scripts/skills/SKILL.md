# Chain of custody

A claim travels with its chain of transmission. Take the one claim your whole
position rests on and trace it back — who says it, citing whom — until you hit
a primary source.

## Method
1. Identify the single claim everything else depends on.
2. Walk the chain backward: who said it, citing whom. Each link is a
   source-to-source step.
3. Test each link twice:
   - Is this source reliable for this kind of claim?
   - What can and cannot be concluded from it?
4. Stop when you hit a primary source (the origin, not a re-statement).
5. Write the chain: claim → intermediate links → primary source, with the
   reliability note per link.

## Rules
- A secondary source is a link, never a terminus.
- Absence of a chain is not authority — "everyone knows" is not a citation.
- If the chain breaks, the claim's strength breaks at that exact link — say
  where, don't paper over it.

## Gotchas
- Stopping at the first plausible source instead of the primary one.
- Confusing "this source repeats the claim" with "this source verified the
  claim."
- Letting agreement with the claim lower the bar on its chain (confirmation
  bias in disguise).
