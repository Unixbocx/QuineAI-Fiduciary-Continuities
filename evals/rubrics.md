# ALOP rubric — 5 anchored dimensions

Scores are 1–5. **0 = cannot tell** (valid, not a failure). Anchors are
binding; a score without anchor support is a guess. Judge every dimension,
then justify the LOWEST score in one sentence.

## D1. Accuracy / fidelity
- 5: Every claim checks out against source; no fabrication, no overreach.
- 3: Mostly accurate; minor imprecision, no invented content.
- 1: Materially wrong or invented content.
- 0: Cannot verify against source.

## D2. Completeness
- 5: Covers everything the task requires, nothing missing.
- 3: Covers the core; some required part thin or absent.
- 1: Large required parts missing.
- 0: Cannot tell what was required.

## D3. Structure / discipline
- 5: Right sections in the right order; no padding, no parroting, no
  scope-creep.
- 3: Mostly structured; some filler or drift.
- 1: Disorganized or padded; answers a different question than asked.
- 0: Cannot tell.

## D4. Depth / warrants
- 5: Load-bearing claims are traced to sources; objections are engaged, not
  dodged.
- 3: Some warrants shown; key claim asserted, not sourced.
- 1: Claims asserted with no chain of custody; no objection handling.
- 0: Cannot tell.

## D5. Cost / efficiency
- 5: Right-size output; nothing redundant, nothing wasted.
- 3: Some waste (verbosity, reruns, unneeded calls).
- 1: Heavy waste for the output delivered.
- 0: Cannot tell.

## Scored report format
```
D1: n (anchor) — phrase
D2: n (anchor) — phrase
D3: n (anchor) — phrase
D4: n (anchor) — phrase
D5: n (anchor) — phrase
LOWEST: Dx — one sentence
```

## Calibration rules
- A "good" artifact is a 3, not a 4. 4 means genuinely excellent on that
  dimension; 5 sets the bar.
- Judge the artifact, not the intent, and never the model you suspect produced
  it.
